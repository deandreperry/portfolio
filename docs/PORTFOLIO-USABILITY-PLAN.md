# Portfolio evaluation plan

Status: prepared, not conducted. No participant feedback or results have been collected.

## Recruit participants

Invite 3–5 people representative of the intended audience: recruiters, product-design managers, and design peers. Obtain their agreement to participate and ask permission separately before recording. Do not send invitations without De'Andre's authorization.

## Session: approximately 15 minutes

Use the published portfolio after real project evidence is present. Include both phone and desktop sessions. Start at the homepage. Explain that the site is being tested, not the participant; avoid coaching.

1. Let the participant explore for 15 seconds, then hide the page. Ask: Who is this person? What role do they do? What stands out? Record their words without correcting them.
2. Allow 30 seconds of browsing. Ask which project they would open and why. Record the first project selected and any hesitation.
3. Ask them to inspect a case study and describe the designer's personal contribution, one evidence-based decision, and the outcome. Record what they find versus infer.
4. Ask them to locate the résumé and a way to contact the designer. Record completion, confusion, and navigation path. Do not require sending a real message.
5. Ask what information increased or reduced their confidence, and what was missing.
6. Observe one keyboard-only pass through navigation and a case-study disclosure; include users of assistive technology in a dedicated follow-up when possible.

## Capture sheet

Participant code | audience role | device | role understood | first project chosen | contribution understood | decision/evidence found | outcome understood | résumé found | contact found | friction | quote with context

Do not convert a small convenience sample into broad statistical claims. Record observed counts with the denominator, distinguish behavior from opinions, and note participant-selection limitations.

## Prioritization

Fix blockers to finding work, understanding contribution, reading evidence, and contacting the designer first. Then address repeated confusion, then preference-level feedback. Retest changed flows with new participants. Preserve conflicting findings rather than forcing agreement.

## Recruiter impression follow-up

After the brief visit ask, without suggesting an answer:
- What do you remember about De'Andre and his work?
- Which project would you inspect first, and why?
- What specifically increases your confidence in his design ability?
- What makes you hesitate or needs supporting evidence?
- Did the interactive study help your assessment? What did you learn from it?

Record exact statements with context. Compare recalled claims with the actual content; distinguish visual preference from misunderstanding. Capture trust-increasing and trust-reducing observations separately. Do not infer a hiring recommendation from interface testing alone.

Participant sessions remain unconducted. The user has not authorized contacting recruiters or supplied participants.
